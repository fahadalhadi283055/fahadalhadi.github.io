Gravity Light – Design and Validation of Sustainable Energy Device

Designed, prototyped, and experimentally validated a gravity-powered lighting system that converts gravitational potential energy into electrical energy using a compact compound planetary transmission and DC motor generator.

Machine Design

Planetary Gear Train

3D Printing (PLA)

Experimental Validation

1) Design Requirements

  • Target drop height: 1800 mm
  • Target descent speed: 1.5 mm/s (target ~20 minutes runtime)
  • Target mass: 12 kg (manual lift/reset)
  • Goal: Generate usable voltage for LED lighting using gravity-driven motion

2) Concept & Key Engineering Decisions

  • Selected a compound planetary gear system to achieve extreme speed multiplication in a compact layout.
  • Iterated gear design for additive manufacturing (FDM): increased gear module for printability and strength.
  • Validated gear tooth strength using Lewis bending equation and selected PLA as printable material.

3) Final Gear Train (Optimized Design)

  • Overall gear ratio: ~2734.7
  • Input speed: ~0.083 RPM (from low-speed gravitational motion)
  • Output speed: ~227 RPM (target motor RPM range)
  • Module: 1.5 (improved tooth strength + printability)
  • Planet ring gear: 115 teeth, ~172.5 mm diameter

4) Material & Structural Validation

  • Material: PLA (FDM compatible, low warping, smooth surface)
  • Max calculated gear bending stress: ~14.47 MPa
  • PLA tensile strength reference: ~40–60 MPa → stress within safe limits

5) Prototype & Experimental Results

  • Gear train assembled and tested: smooth motion, no tooth skipping/binding or visible deformation.
  • Motor selection test:
    • DSMP320-24V: could not be driven under gravity load (starting torque + losses too high).
    • Allied 990412018105-12V: successful gravity-driven operation.
  • Experimental test conditions (validated):
    • Dead weight: 5.4 kg
    • Measured drop speed: ~21 mm/s
    • Motor speed: ~1200 RPM
    • Max voltage: ~2 V
  • Illumination time: ~2 minutes achieved (main limitation: excessive dropping speed)

6) Findings & Improvements

  • Runtime is governed by dropping speed more than total gravitational energy.
  • Rubber-band transmission identified as a major loss source (slip + elastic losses + pre-tension).
  • Proposed optimization:
    • Redesign large pulley to ~120 mm to reduce overall ratio to ~1750.
    • Replace rubber band with timing belt / chain drive / direct gear coupling for better efficiency and speed control.
